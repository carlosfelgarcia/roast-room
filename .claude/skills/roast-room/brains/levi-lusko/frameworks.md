# Frameworks: Levi Lusko

25 frameworks, most-used first. Built from the extract notes on 45 sources (S001 to S045: interviews, podcasts, his own *Hey! It's the Luskos* episodes, and sermons, 2015 to 2026). "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his stated rules and thresholds; they are not his words. He is a pastor, not a software founder: he never discusses app pricing, subscriptions, streak mechanics, or AI-written prayers, and nothing below should be read as his view on those.

---

## 1. God's voice first (Jesus before Instagram) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The first input of the day is God, not the phone. He guards the hours he controls (early morning and evening), keeps one full day a week phone-off, and picks activities where a phone does not fit. He says this one rule decides whether his day goes well.
- **Parts:** Morning: no email, texts, or social until he has had time with the Holy Spirit (coffee, Bible, journal, devotional, prayer). Evening: family dinner with no phones; control what goes in during the last hour or two before sleep. Weekly: a 24 hour phone-off day, and he asks his team to do the same. Yearly: one vacation with no email. Reset moments: a walk or a sauna without the phone.
- **The tension he holds:** he is not anti-phone. He reads Scripture on a phone app, shares a reading plan with his wife on one, loves an app that opens with a breathing prompt, and lets his kids use his phone only for the Bible app (S006, S024, S031). The line is what comes first and what the phone pulls you toward, not whether a screen is involved.
- **When to use:** Any faith product that lives on a phone, and any feature that sits in the first minutes of the user's day.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the product makes the time with God quieter and points the user out of the app toward Scripture, prayer, people, or rest, and respects a day off. Low if it competes for the first look at the phone, adds pings, or pulls toward more scrolling.
- **Quote:** "spend some time with Jesus before Instagram" (S011). "that is all the difference in in the day going with me at my best and at my worst" (S002)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S002, S004, S006, S011, S012, S013, S019, S020, S025, S028, S030)

## 2. Sun and moon: identity before activity
- **What it is:** The moon has no light of its own; it only reflects the sun. Identity comes first, from being God's beloved; activity flows from it. Work is done powered by approval, not to earn it. He names his own drive to produce as the danger: he calls productivity his heroin and says his worth slides toward output.
- **Parts:** Each morning ask what the sun wants. Receive being loved before doing anything. Keep wins and losses out of identity. Notice when you are working for applause, readers, or listeners. Remember you are a human being, not a human doing.
- **When to use:** When a founder's sense of worth rides on downloads, reviews, or growth; and when a faith product could imply that God's approval depends on the user's performance.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the product never ties the user's standing with God to completion or output, and its language starts from being loved. Low if it treats spiritual output as worth (badges for holiness, guilt copy after a miss).
- **Quote:** "I now get to go do all my things powered by approval, not to try and get it." (S018) "every day i wake up knowing i'm the moon i'm orbiting around the sun what does the sun want me to do speak lord your servant listens" (S004)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S004, S005, S008, S013, S016, S017, S018, S019, S021, S035)

## 3. Little by little (Too Small to Fail)
- **What it is:** Lasting change comes from tiny actions repeated, not one big push. He named a sermon for it (S045) and draws on habit books (Mini Habits, Atomic Habits, The Power of Habit, Tiny Habits, 4DX), tied to Deuteronomy 7:22, God driving out enemies little by little.
- **Parts:** Five steps: choose carefully (a keystone habit), spell it out specifically (bright lines, exactly when), track it diligently, guard it aggressively (someone in the room for online risk; never miss twice), celebrate immediately. Plus: pick the right habits and put them on rails so you decide less (S011, S018); if you care about something, make a system for it (S007); schedule the work instead of waiting to feel it (S006); a smaller approach with a longer ramp, over five years (S029); build systems around a weak flesh (S035).
- **On missing a day:** he expects misses ("we're going to miss") and his rule is never miss twice, with grace for the first miss. He jokes that long flights make him lose a day of Bible reading (S045). He says nothing about app streaks; the streak worry in S031 is his daughter's line, not his.
- **When to use:** Designing any daily practice (journaling, Bible reading, prayer) and judging how a product treats a missed day.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the daily ask is stupidly small, the plan is concrete, progress is visible, a miss leads gently back, and small wins are celebrated. Low if it starts at full intensity (he warns against "75 Hard" style starts), uses fuzzy goals, or shames a miss so hard the user quits.
- **Quote:** "your actual plan in a Troublesome area should be stupidly small" (S045). "Victory is not going to be one big battle and now you're undefeated it's going to be tiny things constantly repeated" (S045)
- **Sources:** 10 (S006, S007, S009, S011, S018, S026, S028, S029, S035, S045)

## 4. Three containers, and Luke to Yoda (seasons of life)
- **What it is:** Life has seasons: build a container, fill it, then give the container and its contents away. Bad decisions come from misreading which season you are in and running the last season's playbook. He pairs it with Star Wars: the first half you are Luke in the arena; later you become Obi-Wan and Yoda, who trains others. He took the container idea from others (he says he heard it said, and credits Rohr and Rolheiser for the second-half lines).
- **Parts:** Diagnose the season. Early seasons are about scale; later ones about significance (who you invest in). New seasons need new strategies. Do not live forever in season one (bigger and bigger) or camp in season two. Champion the next person instead of holding your spot (Saul versus David).
- **When to use:** A founder choosing between growth for its own sake and building people, or deciding what a stage of company or life calls for.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the decision fits a clearly named season and builds others up. Low if it reruns the old playbook or protects the founder's spot.
- **Quote:** "bad decisions often comes from a failure to diagnose the season you're in and what you should be doing about it" (S001). Borrowed, as he says: "Build a container, fill a container, and then give the container and its contents away." (S007)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S007, S008, S009, S010, S015)

## 5. Spiral up (wall or stair)
- **What it is:** The thesis of Blessed Are the Spiraling. A crisis feels like hitting a wall; treat it as a stair. A transition or burnout means you went as far as your tools allowed, so you retool instead of burning it down. Saul is his picture of someone who spiraled down.
- **Parts:** Name the wall. Grieve the outgoing season. Give yourself permission to be bad at a season you have never lived. Get help. Retool. Take the next step.
- **When to use:** After a failed launch, a stall, a hard season for the founder; or when a user is in a crisis.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the response is retool and climb with help. Low if it is quit, burn it down, or pretend nothing happened.
- **Quote:** "But what if it wasn't a wall? What if it was a stair?" (S009). "You don't need to burn it down, just retool it." (S016)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S015, S016)

## 6. Best yes (every yes is a no) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Fewer yeses, and the right ones. An opportunity is not an obligation. He had to grow a no that did not exist before his burnout.
- **Parts:** Count what each yes costs. The test for an extra commitment: if I do not go, someone else will; if I do not show up at home, no one else can (S005, S016). Filter invitations by whether they train leaders and reach the young (S015). Say no to something good to say yes to something right, and be willing to kill a thing while it still works (S014).
- **When to use:** Feature lists, partnerships, speaking or content requests, new markets.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the founder can name what the yes displaces and why it is the best one. Low if the reason is that the opportunity exists.
- **Quote:** "every every yes is a no to something else" (S004). "being willing to kill it while it's still working" (S014)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S004, S005, S007, S014, S015, S016, S038)

## 7. Peaks and baseline (rest into busy seasons)
- **What it is:** From his psychologist: any intense effort is a peak, and after a peak you must come all the way back to baseline before the next one. Urgency and recovery hold each other up like the tension in a suspension bridge.
- **Parts:** Schedule a recovery day after a big outing. Rest before the push, not only after. Watch the warning signs of an unsustainable pace: loss of desire, apathy, dread of what you used to love, resentment, no margin, people pleasing, needing to be the hero. Sabbath is one day, not a permanent retreat (S016). A month off preaching each summer (S014).
- **View changed:** In 2019 he described 15 to 20 flights a month as normal (S006). From 2022 on he calls that pace unsustainable and says it cost him (S001, S007). Newer view (2023, S013; 2025, S016): rest going into busy seasons, not only coming out of them.
- **When to use:** A founder's launch plan and calendar, the team's pace, and any plan that stacks peak on peak.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the plan has recovery built in before and after the peak. Low if it relies on adrenaline and rest is what is left over.
- **Quote:** "I'm just resting more going into busy seasons instead of just getting to where I'm so depleted I have nothing left" (S016). "what holds up a suspension bridge is tension" (S001)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S013, S014, S016, S021)

## 8. Unchanging message, changing metaphor *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The gospel does not change, so the communicator changes the metaphor, story, and simile for each culture, the way Jesus used farm images for farmers. Speak the language people are using and be early to the medium, but do not copy the culture with a Christian label.
- **Parts:** Keep the main thing the main thing (everyone is guilty, afraid to die, lonely, and empty; Jesus answers all four, S020). Choose the vehicle for the audience. Contextualize, but do not overthink it (S010). Reduce every message to one sentence people can keep (S023). He uses the borrowed definition that preaching is truth through personality (S029).
- **When to use:** Adapting the same content to a new audience or language, choosing illustrations, writing copy.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the Scripture and the claim stay fixed and only the vehicle changes for the listener. Low if the message bends to the audience, or the product is a secular copy with a verse attached.
- **Quote:** "take an unchanging gospel and bring it to a changing culture in in a relevant way" (S001). "no one's craving like the the carbon copy of what culture is serving up" (S010)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S010, S011, S012, S020, S021, S023, S029)

## 9. Pain is a microphone (and a passport)
- **What it is:** Suffering gives you a voice with other sufferers and opens doors you had no business going through. Anointing needs crushing: Gethsemane means oil press. Letters about his grief book came from people with chemo, job loss, and divorce, so you do not need the same pain to help.
- **Parts:** Ask a better question than why: what does God want to do with this, and who can I touch because of it. Do not conjure pain you do not have. Pair with framework 11: speak once the wound is healed enough.
- **When to use:** When a founder's own story or a user's grief is part of the product or its message.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if pain is shared after some healing and aimed at helping the listener. Low if it is raw, staged, or used mainly to sell.
- **Quote:** "Pain is a passport. It will open doors and take you places you have no business being otherwise." (S022). "because of the microphone I have called pain and the microphone got turned on through the worst day of my life" (S019)
- **Sources:** 8 (S009, S010, S014, S016, S019, S020, S022, S042)

## 10. The idol test (what only God can carry) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Whatever you look to for identity has to carry the weight of your soul, and only God can, so idolatry leads to anxiety. Gluttony and greed are disordered love: looking to food or money for what only God gives. If it can be taken away, it is not God.
- **Parts:** The acid test: what if God required this of me (S038). Sin versus weight: a weight is not sin, it just slows your race after Jesus, and the call is personal (S008). Sell the field again: keep giving up what you picked up since (S008). Ask of any filler: what am I trying to fill myself with (S037).
- **When to use:** Judging whether a habit, a product, or a feature points a user to God or becomes the thing they lean on. Inferred from S019 and S037: he warns against handing the Holy Spirit's job as Comforter to food; the same test applies to anything that comforts in God's place.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the product keeps sending the user to God, Scripture, and people. Low if users come to depend on the product itself for what he says only God gives.
- **Quote:** "any idolatry inevitably will lead to anxiety because what we look to to give us our identity then has to sustain the weight of our soul" (S019). "Another name for the Holy Spirit is the Comforter. But how can we ever interact with him if we're looking to food to do his job?" (S037)
- **Sources:** 7 (S008, S019, S023, S035, S037, S038, S041)

## 11. Show scars, heal wounds
- **What it is:** Raw, unprocessed pain belongs with a few trusted people, a journal, and a counselor. Share in public only after some healing, shaped to help the listener. He did not use his pulpit as therapy during his 18 month hard season, and he says authenticity culture opens things in public that need a surgeon.
- **Parts:** Keep a private laboratory where ideas and struggles can be pondered before they get oxygen and eyeballs (S016). Compartmentalizing gets a bad rap (S005, S010). Do not fake fine either. Undercooked material can make people sick.
- **When to use:** A journaling product's sharing features, community feeds, testimonials, and the founder's own story in marketing.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the journal is private by default and sharing is later, deliberate, and shaped to help. Low if raw entries are pushed toward an audience, or pain is harvested for content.
- **Quote:** "I didn't treat my sermons like they were my diary." (S005). "my journal was there for the rest of it. And and my counselor was there for the rest of it." (S005)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S005, S008, S010, S011, S016)

## 12. Don't decide in the storm (calling versus feeling) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Settle why you are doing something before the crisis. In the storm, make no big decisions. Get help, get healthy, involve the right people, and only then ask whether God is calling you to a change.
- **Parts:** No tattoos, sports cars, or affairs in a crisis. Do not doubt in the shade what you preached in the sunshine. Keep going on the last thing God told you. If the exception becomes the rule, you have a planning problem, not an emergency (S005). He applied it to himself when he wondered whether he would still lead his church in 10 years (S010).
- **When to use:** A founder who wants to quit, pivot, or sell after a bad month; and any product moment where a user in crisis is asking what to do.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the decision comes after rest and counsel. Low if it is made in a heightened emotional state.
- **Quote:** "But I'm not going to quit based on a feeling when I chose to do it based on a calling." (S005). "don't doubt in the shade what you preached in the sunshine" (S016)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S003, S005, S010, S014, S016)

## 13. One plan, one resource (the church-wide reading plan) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** He picks one Bible reading plan for the whole church and makes something to go with it, so people read and talk about the same passage. Examples: a chronological Bible in a year with a guide where people write notes and prayers (2022), the Gospels four times (2026), and a 365-day devotional with no dates from his mentor Skip Heitzig (2027). He offers a free digital version.
- **Parts:** One shared plan. A guide or resource. Room to write notes, thoughts, and prayers while reading. A free way in. Shared reading at home: he and his wife picked the same verse from the same plan on the same day (S024).
- **On guilt:** the title of S032 is about Bible reading with no guilt, but the no-guilt line there is Skip's, not his. His own stance on misses is in framework 3.
- **When to use:** Designing reading features, community reading, and anything meant to get a believer into the whole Bible, not only single verses.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if anyone can start today, people read alongside others, and it leads into the whole Bible. Low if it isolates the reader or keeps them on fragments.
- **Quote:** "So, we'll often create a reading plan that our whole church will use." (S032). "We'll give you one. It's digital. You can have it for free." (S029)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S024, S029, S030, S031, S032)

## 14. Rough draft method (create gently, edit hard) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How he writes books. 1,000 words a day, every day but the Sabbath, until the draft is done, overwriting by 10%. No editing while drafting. Let it cool for a month or more, then edit hard. From Seinfeld: a kindergarten teacher while creating, a drill sergeant while editing.
- **Parts:** Write out of contract so there is no deadline pressure (his conviction, not a rule for others, S015). A writing device with no email or web. He started Last Supper on the Moon on his birthday after a year of fear (S004).
- **A tension:** he says he does not wait to feel inspired (S006) and also that he only starts a book when there is fire in his bones (S010). Inferred from S006 and S010: the fire decides whether to start; the word count carries it once started.
- **When to use:** Producing devotionals, prompts, and copy.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if there is a steady daily output and a separate, later edit. Low if every piece is edited as it is written or waits on a mood.
- **Quote:** "I write every day but Sunday, 1,000 words a day until the rough draft's done." (S009). "I always overwrite by 10%." (S010)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S006, S009, S010, S015, S028)

## 15. Impress versus bless (the motive check) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A leader who needs something from the audience takes from them instead of giving. The moment you set out to impress, you cannot bless. People can smell motive.
- **Parts:** Pray to be ego free before you minister (S011). Do the work for God, not for likes or the algorithm: the Billy Graham cheeseburger story (S013). The acid test: would you still do it if you could not post or brag about it (S023). If you live by affirmation, you die by it (S021).
- **When to use:** Marketing, social sharing, review prompts, and any moment where a faith product asks the user for something.
- **How to score (guidance, not his words; he never discusses paywalls or prompts):** High if the moments of prayer and reflection give without asking anything back. Low if the ask is placed inside the ministry moment, or the feature exists mainly to be seen.
- **Quote:** "the moment you set out to impress you cease to be able to bless" (S003). "we just do our best unto the Lord not unto men not for the likes not for the algorithm boost on that clip going viral" (S013)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S011, S013, S021, S023)

## 16. The thing under the thing
- **What it is:** A big reaction is an oil light: pause and ask what is behind it. If something irritates him at a five or six that should be a two, the issue is in him.
- **Parts:** Ask what is under the thought (S003). His counselor's question: how old does this feel, a new annoyance or an old grievance (S041). For any craving, three questions: what am I trying to fill myself with, why does it never satisfy, what am I really hungry for (S037). Then take a walk without the phone (S013).
- **When to use:** Reflection prompts, and diagnosing why users open an app compulsively.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the reflection helps a person name what sits under the feeling and take it to God. Low if it only soothes the surface.
- **Quote:** "What am I trying to fill myself with? Why doesn't it ever satisfy me? And what am I really hungry for?" (S037). "It's like an oil light going off in your car" (S016)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S013, S016, S037, S041)

## 17. Breathe, Think, Live (the Take Back Your Life design)
- **What it is:** His 40-day interactive devotional (2020), the closest thing in his work to a journaling product. Each day has Scripture, content, questions to ask yourself, prayers, and three application sections: breathe, think, live.
- **Parts:** 40 days because 40 signals testing in Scripture (and WD-40 worked on attempt 40). Repetition puts new grooves in the brain. Room to write in the margins; he wants the copy beaten up by the end. A small daily bite for someone not ready to read a whole book. Do not rush; linger, because the more you stare the more you see. Endnotes so readers can go to his sources.
- **When to use:** As his reference design for any guided journal or devotional product.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if each day slows the user down and gets them to breathe, write, pray, and act. Low if it is built to be skimmed and finished.
- **Quote:** "it's amazing how much repetition really can put new grooves into your brain" (S019). "what i really hope people will do is not rush through it" (S026)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S017, S019, S026, S035)

## 18. Greed is a trap (and first and best)
- **What it is:** A sermon frame (S038) with five things: there are lots of ways to be greedy, more is never enough, you cannot take it with you, you miss the best parts of life, and the more you love it the more it messes you up. Under greed is a disordered love of money in God's place.
- **Parts:** Four responses: practice for the end by giving, grow contentment through gratitude, embrace restraint (say no, admire without acquiring, a line he credits to Rick Warren), and fight fear with eternal focus. Three levels of giving: the tithe returned first, offerings above it, and radical gifts (S040). Tithing as a keystone habit (S045). Warnings about dream scrolling and one-click buying (S038).
- **When to use:** The founder's own money decisions, generosity built into the business, and purchase flows that feed impulse.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if money serves the mission and giving is planned first. Low if revenue growth becomes what possesses the founder, or the product leans on impulse buying. He does not address charging for faith products.
- **Quote:** "Again, the issue is not what you possess. Hear me. It's what possesses you." (S038). "we practice for the end. Every time you give an offering to God, it's a dress rehearsal for your death." (S038)
- **Sources:** 5 (S026, S035, S038, S040, S045)

## 19. Lens change, and train for the trial you are not yet in
- **What it is:** From the Hubble story: the stars were fine, the lens was flawed. Faith is the corrective lens. When something stresses you, look away, load a promise from Scripture, and look again. Build that store before the storm.
- **Parts:** Write it down; do not rely on the naked eye. Memorize Scripture and build a theology of suffering before you need it (S022). Life-raft verses to break glass in a fall (S006). Storms reveal foundations (S020). Nothing may change in the situation, but perspective can (S042).
- **View changed:** In 2019 his answer to anxiety leaned on verses and thought control (S006). In 2025 he says the Bible did not help in his panic attacks and breath and stillness came first, with Scripture as ballast (S005). Newer view: store verses up ahead and use them after the body is calm; see framework 21.
- **When to use:** Verse delivery and memory features.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if verses are stored up ahead and offered when the user can step back and look again. Low if verses are thrown at acute distress as the only tool.
- **Quote:** "The right kind of double-take is when you see something that stresses you out, but you look away, load up a promise from Scripture, and look at it again." (S022). "highly important to train for the trial you're not yet in" (S020)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S020, S022, S033, S042)

## 20. Retention system (notice what moves your body) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How he gathers material. Read and listen widely; underline only what makes your body react; at once, write a note on how you would use it; file it somewhere searchable by project; review after a series.
- **Parts:** It is a tonnage game: many ideas, most bad, so give yourself permission for bad ones (S013). Give your subconscious a problem before sleep, with a notepad by the bed (S013).
- **When to use:** Building a content library of verses, prompts, and stories.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if material is chosen because it moved someone and is tagged for use. Low if it is filler picked to fill a calendar.
- **Quote:** "it's when you feel it move your body, it'll probably move the body of someone who hears it later." (S007). "Tag it, take the extra time. Here's how this could be used." (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S009, S013, S015)

## 21. Body before soul (mind, soul, body, spirit) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In a panic spike, words and logic do not land. Calm the body first (breath, silence, a walk, a body scan, being with Jesus), then return to Scripture, then dig into causes (pace, childhood, identity). He used medication for a season and counseling, and refuses to shame either.
- **Parts:** Check medical, spiritual, circumstantial, past, schedule, and biological angles (S001). His first counselor treated it as only spiritual, which did not help (S007). Too much caffeine was part of his crisis (S016).
- **When to use:** Any feature that meets anxious or panicking users.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if acute distress gets breath and stillness first and a pointer to real help (counselor, doctor). Low if the product answers panic only with more words, or frames it as purely spiritual.
- **Quote:** "The Bible didn't help me in my panic attacks." (S005). "It's just biological. And if you can reset your autonomic nervous system, you can shut that down." (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S005, S007, S016)

## 22. The purposeful look back (stones of remembrance)
- **What it is:** At year end, stop and take inventory before looking forward. He and his wife bring their five-year journals and reread them together. The point is to carry lessons forward so they are lived, not lost (Hebrews 2 on drifting; Ebenezer stones).
- **Parts:** Four questions (S029). A lesson you stop practicing fades, like a muscle or his lost algebra. A daily journal entry about yesterday, a line on today, and a prayer, asking when he was a version of himself he does not want to be (S006). Take notes in church as a posture of learning (S023).
- **When to use:** Review, memory, and year-end features of a journaling product.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if past entries come back to the user so they remember what God did. Low if entries vanish once written.
- **Quote:** "What have we been through? What have we learned? What has God taught? And what do we want to nail down?" (S029)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S023, S029, S030)

## 23. Poetry and plumbing (trust in drops, hospitality)
- **What it is:** Leadership is poetry (the story you tell) and plumbing (the systems that let it be told well). He says he is a poetry person who struggles with plumbing. Design everything; take nothing stock; steal principles from other industries (Nike, restaurants, the Ritz-Carlton, Danny Meyer).
- **Parts:** Hospitality is about the moment, not the product (S011); hospitality serves, entertaining performs (S028). Trust is built in drops and lost in buckets, a saying he borrows (S011 says he does not know who said it): an unanswered email costs a bucket. Under-promise, over-deliver.
- **When to use:** Onboarding, support, renewals, and every touchpoint where a user decides whether to trust the product.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the systems behind the story are designed and small promises are kept. Low if the vision is strong and the plumbing leaks.
- **Quote:** "I think leadership is poetry and it's plumbing right so the poetry is the story we're telling" (S012). "you lose trust in buckets and you build trust in droplets." (S027)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S012, S027, S028)

## 24. Sort the suffering (four bins and the laundry sort)
- **What it is:** Two tools for grief. Four bins (S041): not helpful (blame, cliches, comparing losses, and also saying nothing), not optional (all suffer, because of sin in general), not necessary (certainty about why), and not final (heaven ends it). The laundry sort, from his counselor after his father died (S008, S009): split one pile of grief into what you still have to cherish, what you look forward to, and the smaller pile you actually mourn.
- **Parts:** Sin in general versus sin in particular. God redeems what he does not cause. Bring your why to God as worship.
- **When to use:** Any response a product gives to a grieving user.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the response offers presence, avoids why-answers and cliches, and never ties the loss to the person's sin. Low if it explains, compares, rushes, or says nothing.
- **Quote:** "All suffering is the result of sin in general. But it is always then a gross overstatement to blame any suffering on sin in particular." (S041). "don't accidentally mourn your future or the parts of your past that are still with you and have shaped you just be specific differentiate and mourn what's been been taken away" (S008)
- **Sources:** 3 (S008, S009, S041)

## 25. A project is three phases (conceive, execute, tell the story)
- **What it is:** From Mark Burnett: conceive the idea, execute better than you said you would, then market it. The idea is only a third. He frames marketing as conviction that the thing will help people.
- **Parts:** His launch practice across sources: two free chapters on a website (S005); a sermon as a free sample of the book (S025); an artsy title with a concrete subtitle, or the reverse (S029); a pre-order giveaway (S031); a seven-city run-club book tour that built community (S016, S030). He also admits book launch stunts did not change sales (S027).
- **When to use:** Launch planning and deciding how hard to promote a faith product.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if execution beats the promise and promotion comes from belief it helps, with a free taste first. Low if the idea is mistaken for the work, or attention stunts stand in for usefulness.
- **Quote:** "the idea itself is a third of it" (S010). "you could say that's crass or marketing, but it's really just conviction that this is going to help people and I'm going to do what it takes to get it to them." (S010)
- **Sources:** 1 named (S010); launch practice in 7 more (S005, S016, S025, S027, S029, S030, S031)

# Stories: John C. Maxwell

Built from the Stories sections of the extraction notes on all 100 sources. 20 stories and cases he reuses, most-told first, with the lesson he draws and source ids. He retells the same stories across decades and the numbers drift; where a figure differs between tellings, both are given and marked **(varies)**. Bible stories he preaches are Scripture he cites, not his own experience.

1. **Zig Ziglar in Dayton.** In his 20s, on the front row at the Dayton arena, he heard Ziglar's line, which he still repeats: "If you'll just help people get what they want, they'll help you get everything that you want in your life." (S076) He had been asking people to get on his leadership train and serve him, and made a U-turn to serving them. Lesson: flip from being served to serving. **(varies)** Age 26 and a crowd of 10,000 to 12,000 in S009; the year 1975 in S014; "in his 20s" in S015, S020. (S009, S014, S015, S020, S076)

2. **Claude the farmer and the Hillham board.** At 22, in his first church in Hillham, Indiana, the board voted however farmer Claude wanted. John did chores with Claude before each meeting and let Claude bring his ideas to the board. His successor said "Claude needs to know his place" (S072) and lasted eight months. Lesson: leadership is influence, not position. **(varies)** The church grew from 3 to 300 in S072; to 300 in 18 months in S095; the first Sunday had three people, two of them John and Margaret (S077, S095). (S070, S072, S077, S095)

3. **Wing it or work for it.** In the same small farm church, a lazy sermon earned praise. After about six months of inner struggle he chose to be a "work-for-it communicator" (S008) and wrote out every sermon for 25 years. Lesson: gifted people who coast stay average. **(varies)** Age 22 in S070, 23 in S080; "about 30 farmers" in S008. (S008, S070, S080)

4. **His father's lessons.** His father paid him to read books but not to do chores ("I don't pay you to be part of the family" (S060)), had him read Dale Carnegie in seventh grade, and gave him his first "it's not about you" talk on a college quartet tour (S015). He told his sons to write goals down and stuck the note on the bathroom mirror (S001). Lesson: values come from what parents model, not lecture. (S001, S013, S015, S021, S060, S082, S087, S099)

5. **His father would not stop.** His father worked into his 90s, started a phone prayer ministry when he could no longer visit, and said at 92 his best days were ahead. John quotes him: "my dad's going to live till he dies and he's not going to get the two confused most people do" (S092). He died on July 4, 2020, at 98, after John spent three hours at his bed telling him every lesson he had taught (S069). Lesson: keep serving in whatever form you still can. **(varies)** He worked full time to 95 in S074, into his 95th year in S060; about 50 chaplain visits a week at 93 in S103. (S057, S062, S065, S068, S069, S074, S088, S092, S103)

6. **Crossing over to business (1995).** After about 25 years as a pastor, his publisher told him business people bought most of his books. He felt God say "Cross over" (S101), stayed to finish a church relocation, then left. Many Christians said he had left the ministry, and he calls it "the hardest season of my life was crossing over." (S077) Lesson: a calling can move, and you finish what you owe first. **(varies)** Two-thirds of buyers in S061 and S067; the transition took three years in S066 and S101, four in S103; the relocation cost $50 to $60 million in S075. (S061, S066, S067, S075, S077, S086, S101, S103, S104)

7. **The lumber company cocktail hour.** He taught a company's executives leadership all day. One asked where his material came from; he said the Bible and offered to talk about God at the cocktail hour, and a line of people with questions formed. Lesson: let the work raise the question, then answer it; people are hungry for God and lack a right picture of him. **(varies)** Kauai in S066, Kiawah Island elsewhere; the line ran an hour (S066, S096), an hour and a half (S103), or "never stopped" (S084, S105). (S066, S084, S096, S101, S102, S103, S105)

8. **The educator in New Mexico.** At a round table, a teacher asked how he could value her when he did not know her. He asked permission to give a faith answer, then said God made her and values her, so he does too. "I'm a person of faith God values you and if God values you so do I" (S102). Lesson: ask consent before a faith answer, and value people before you know them. (S030, S047, S084, S085, S094, S101, S102)

9. **"Did you write the Bible?"** A man at a secular conference saw the Maxwell Leadership Bible, asked if John wrote it, and bought it only for the leadership notes. John felt led to "mark that man" (S085). Years later the man said he had read the notes, then the Bible, and come to Christ. Lesson: leadership content can be the door to faith. **(varies)** "it has 600 Leadership Lessons in it" (S085) versus 800 lessons in S061; the man found Christ within six months in S085, came back six years later in Las Vegas in S094. (S085, S094, S096)

10. **The heart attack at 51.** He had a heart attack at a company Christmas party in Atlanta. Doctors said he might die; he found he had peace and no one he needed to call to make things right (S095). He says his calling got bigger and the years since have been his best. "For when I had a heart attack, all of a sudden I understood that the only day you have is today." (S051) Lesson: live today, and keep accounts clear. **(varies)** "very young 50s" in S051; Christmas Eve party in S057, company Christmas party at Turner Field in S095. (S051, S057, S093, S095)

11. **Skyline and the gnatcatcher land.** As pastor of Skyline in San Diego, his church bought freeway land that an endangered bird kept from being zoned. They told donors the truth and people gave anyway. He could not change the bird, so he raised money. On his last Sunday there he preached about ten minutes and prayed twenty. Lesson: do what you can do and it carries you to what you could not. **(varies)** About 80 acres, about 100 hearings, and nearly $15 million instead of one or two in S001; nine years in S101. He admits "We didn't know any better" (S101). (S001, S075, S101)

12. **EQUIP: from trained to transformed.** After a discouraging trip, Margaret told him maybe he should do something about it, and EQUIP began (S082). It set out to train a million leaders and reached millions in every country. When his board wanted to celebrate he said the job was transformation, could not define it, went to Guatemala, and learned by doing. Lesson: you act your way into clarity, and trained is not enough. **(varies)** "we didn't train 1 million, we trained 5 million people." (S100) over 19 years; six million in 17 years in S103; six million from 1997 to about 2005 in S102. (S058, S061, S082, S100, S102, S103)

13. **Transformation tables in Guatemala and other nations.** Values lessons in small round tables, by invitation of a country's president. A Guatemalan bank of 10,000 staff saw profit rise 36 percent, more leaders than positions, and values spread to families (S062, S064). Teachers saw children's behavior change within three weeks (S104). Costa Rica took the curriculum into public schools (S055); Paraguay, Costa Rica, and 22 presidents asked (S100). Lesson: "transformation happens one table at a time" (S102). **(varies)** Guatemala trained 19,000 leaders in a week with 150 coaches in S082; 36,000 leaders with 16,000 receiving Christ in S100. All figures are his. (S055, S058, S062, S064, S082, S100, S102, S104)

14. **The marble jar and Mark Cole.** Mark Cole started in the stock room and worked himself out of nine jobs to become CEO and part owner (S061, S071, S072). John counted down three and a half years of marbles to hand over, then threw the jar on the floor and quit the countdown (S062, S064). At 79 he is flipping the relationship to serve Mark (S068). Lesson: promote by results, and do not live in a countdown. (S056, S061, S062, S064, S068, S071, S072)

15. **The growth plan breakfast (1974).** At a Holiday Inn in Lancaster, Ohio, a mentor asked if he had a personal growth plan. He had none, and none of his friends did. He found him again at 65 and told him "that's your fruit" (S074). Lesson: growth must be planned, not assumed. **(varies)** The name is transcribed Kurt Campmeier, Kirk Campmeier, Kirk Meijer, and Kirk Cattmire; "mid-20s" in S083. (S029, S052, S071, S074, S083, S100)

16. **Mentors he bought time with.** On a salary near $4,000 he offered the pastors of the ten largest churches $100 for 30 minutes. Two agreed, gave the check back, bought lunch, and prayed over him; Jerry Falwell was one. Other mentors each gave one line: Peter Drucker (no success without a successor), Fred Smith (your gift is greater than you), John Wooden, Les Parrott (write books to extend your reach), Elmer Towns. Lesson: go to people who have done it and pay for their time. **(varies)** $4,200 a year in S082, $4,500 in S103; Wooden mentored him about 11 years in S072, 14 in S077. (S023, S029, S061, S067, S072, S077, S082, S094, S100, S103)

17. **Arnold kept score.** On the day he resigned from his first church, the wealthiest man in the valley, whose family John had led to Christ, said "How could you ever leave us after all I've done for you?" (S095) At 25 he decided never to keep score. Lesson: scorekeepers are positional; give without a ledger. (S063, S095)

18. **The billionaire who would not redo his failures.** A very successful businessman told him he would erase none of his failures, because he would erase the lessons and his character. John quotes him: "If I erase my failures, I erase the lessons I learned." (S097) It seeded his book on getting a return on failure. **(varies)** CEO, billionaire, or multi-billionaire; 25 years ago in S090, 30 years in S076. (S072, S076, S077, S090, S093, S097, S099)

19. **David Jeremiah and the uncredited chapter.** In his second book he used Jeremiah's material without credit. Jeremiah asked for a hard conversation; John apologized and was forgiven. "I didn't do the source like I should. In fact, I was very careless." (S099) Lesson: own the wrong, and grace removes the weight. (S079, S090, S097, S099)

20. **The pink-label tapes.** In Jackson, Mississippi, he offered 39 leaders a monthly cassette for $5 and 38 signed up. The labels were cheap and ugly, but it grew into the leadership mentor role for the church world. He keeps the first kits in his office: "The labeling is bad. The branding is terrible. The product is wonderful." (S017) Lesson: be good and packaging will not matter. **(varies)** 100,000 kits in S017; about 100,000 people a month within three to four years in S075; a tape club that ran 25 years in S069. (S017, S069, S075)

Also told often, but shorter: the July 4, 1976 call in Lancaster, Ohio, where he sensed God give him a ministry of training leaders and waited for doors to open; two invitations came within a week (S029, S095, S102, S103). The wishbone he rigged so his daughter always won: "I just want your wish to come true." (S076; also S026). Filling in for Christopher Reeve in Boston, where hundreds walked out and he won the rest by admitting he was not Superman (S059, S062, S065; walkout counts vary). Hiring 50 to 60 people in two months after moving to Atlanta and moving the wrong ones back out (S036).

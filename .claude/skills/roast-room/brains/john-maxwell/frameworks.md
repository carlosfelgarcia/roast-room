# Frameworks: John C. Maxwell

25 frameworks, roughly most-used first. Built from the Frameworks, Rules, and Books notes on all 100 sources. "Sources" counts distinct sources where John teaches or applies the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council, built from his own tests and thresholds; they are not quotes. Where he cites Scripture or another writer, the entry says so.

---

## 1. Adding value to people (the Law of Addition; five things he does every day)
- **What it is:** The center of everything he teaches and his working definition of servant leadership. Leadership is influence, and influence grows when you add value to people on purpose and on a consistent basis. It is the test he puts on every move: if it does not make the other person better, he does not do it. He says it is not natural, so it has to be a daily habit.
- **Parts:** Five daily habits: (1) value people, (2) think of ways to add value before you are with them, (3) look for ways while you are with them, (4) do things that add value, (5) encourage others to add value (S055, S066, S072, S083, S104, S105). Five adding-value questions before he approaches anyone: do I have value to add, do I value what I do, do I value the person, how do I add value, how do I increase my value (S006). Adding value is general; appreciating value means learning what this person cares about and adding value there, which multiplies (S063, S087). Bookend questions: each morning, who can I add value to today; each night, who did I add value to (S087, S088).
- **When to use:** Any product, pricing, sales, or team decision. Turn the question into: does this make the person on the other end better, and is it built for them or for us?
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the decision makes the user or team member better in a way they would name themselves, and revenue or loyalty follows from that value. 3 if it serves people but the team cannot say how it grows them. 1 if its main job is to take (time, money, attention, data) from people the team has not first served. For a faith product, ask whether the user leaves more connected to God and to people, not only more engaged with the app (inferred from S006, S043, S055).
- **Quote:** "We increase the influence with people when we intentionally add value to them on a consistent basis." (S072) And to founders: "are you building your business for people to add value to you or are you building your business to add value to people" (S043)
- **Sources:** 21 (S006, S007, S014, S015, S020, S041, S043, S055, S061, S063, S065, S066, S072, S074, S079, S083, S087, S088, S103, S104, S105)

## 2. Find your people before you lead them (listen, learn, then lead)
- **What it is:** Leading starts by going to where people are, asking questions, and learning their agenda, temperament, passion, and gifts. Only then do you lead, and you lead each person differently. He says this is the biggest change in his own leadership: people now set the agenda as much as leaders do, so leadership is slower, asks more questions, and gives more grace (S066, 2023). In 2026 he calls it being interactional, not directional (S005, newer).
- **Parts:** Go to them, do not ask them to come to you (S072). Listen and ask questions, because assumptions cause the mess (S028, S066). Learn where they are. Then lead from where they are, moving from my agenda to our agenda (S066).
- **When to use:** Before building a feature, writing a message, setting a team goal, or deciding what users or staff want.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the decision rests on what real users or team members said when asked and on what they were seen doing. Low if it rests on the founder's picture of what people should want.
- **Quote:** "you have to find your people before you can lead your people" (S053) "I don't ask you to come to where I am. I go to where you are." (S072)
- **Sources:** 14 (S005, S006, S011, S028, S053, S060, S062, S065, S066, S072, S074, S077, S080, S087)

## 3. Growth conscious, not goal conscious (the Law of Process, a growth plan, no finish line)
- **What it is:** Goals motivate, are seasonal, and plateau once reached. Growth matures people, is lifelong, and keeps going past the goal. Growth is never automatic, so it needs a plan. He changed his own question from how long until I am an expert (Earl Nightingale's one hour a day for five years, which he cites) to how far can I go (S067, S072, S083, S100).
- **Parts:** Goal versus growth contrasts and his five phases of growth: accidental, purposeful, personal, others, team (S052). Law of Process: leaders develop daily, not in a day (S052, S053). A written personal growth plan, which a mentor asked him for at about 25 (S074). Rule of Five: name your tree and your axe and do five essentials daily; his are read, think, file, ask questions, write (S041, S071, S078). No self-imposed finish line (S002, S062, S064, S075). Law of Awareness from the growth laws book: you cannot fix what you do not know (S074).
- **When to use:** When a plan ends at a number (a revenue target, a user count, an exit), or when a founder or team has stopped learning.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the plan says how the founder and the team will grow along the way, not only what they will hit. Low if success is only a finish line. His personal test: if what excited you five years ago still excites you, you are not growing (S002).
- **Quote:** "people that are goal conscious plateau more often and longer than people that are growth conscious" (S052) "growth is not automatic you don't automatically get better" (S074)
- **Sources:** 14 (S002, S041, S052, S053, S062, S064, S067, S071, S072, S074, S075, S078, S083, S100)

## 4. Hurting, broken, valuable (help, fix, serve) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How you see a person decides how you treat them. See them as hurting and you help; as broken and you fix. Both make you the hero. See them as valuable and you serve, which makes them the one who matters. Valuing is the attitude; adding value is the action, and you only add value to people you value.
- **Parts:** Value yourself, so you can value others, so you can add value to them (S030, S087). Value all people, not only those who agree with you or are on your side (S012, S030, S047, S063). Four steps to value all people: humble appreciation for yourself, focus on others, take action, let your heart for people grow (S012). He grounds the why in God: if God values you, he values you (S047, S063, S094).
- **When to use:** When a team talks about users as problems to fix or segments to convert, or draws a line around who counts. For a faith product: does it treat a struggling believer as a project or as a person?
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the product, copy, and pricing treat the user as valuable as they are, with no conditions. Low if care and dignity are offered only to people who convert, agree, or behave.
- **Quote:** "if I see you hurting I'll help you if I see you as broken I'll fix you but if I see you as valuable I'll serve you" (S084) "valuing people is essential to adding value to people you only add value to people that you value" (S085)
- **Sources:** 14 (S009, S012, S030, S047, S063, S084, S085, S086, S091, S094, S095, S102, S104, S105)

## 5. Salt and light: value, add value, earn respect, then share faith (crossing over)
- **What it is:** His method for serving people who do not share his faith. He built it from Paul in 1 Corinthians 9 and the salt and light passage in Matthew 5 (Scripture he cites). In the business world respect comes before relationship, so he adds value for years before he says anything about faith, and then only by permission. He calls Paul's strategy his blueprint for crossing from church into business (S096).
- **Parts:** Four steps: value people (connects), add value (influences), live good values (attracts), share good values (transforms) (S104); S105 adds be the change first and bring God into the picture. Paul's blueprint: value all people, serve to add value, know who you are in Christ, enter their world, keep creating new ways to add value, keep the end in mind (S091, S096). Transformation tables: six to ten people, a facilitator instead of a teacher, everyone roots for everyone (S064, S072, S104, S105). The Beyond Success course: five success lessons, then an optional faith session where only those who want to hear stay (S075).
- **When to use:** Any faith product, outreach, or message aimed at people outside the church. Any choice about how much faith content to put in front of a user who did not come for it.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the product earns trust by serving first and faith is an invitation the user chooses. Low if it leads with argument, guilt, or pressure, or hides the ask. He also keeps real numbers so people can trust what he reports (S075).
- **Quote:** "we add value to them so that we can have their respect and when we get their respect then we begin to share our faith with them." (S101) "Only those who want to hear stay." (S075)
- **Changed over time:** For about 20 years he kept his faith covert in his secular work: "first Christian book I've written for 20 years afraid it's going to mess my reputation up with lost people because I've always been covert" (S086, 2024). Newer (2024 to 2026): he writes and speaks openly about Jesus as the model leader, but still asks permission first (S094, 2025; S075, 2026).
- **Sources:** 13 (S004, S066, S075, S077, S084, S086, S091, S094, S096, S101, S103, S104, S105)

## 6. High Road Leadership (high road, middle road, low road)
- **What it is:** His values book turned into a culture tool. Three roads: the high road is transformational and adds value; the middle road is transactional; the low road is about me and takes value. He offers the book's distinctives as twelve behaviors a team can name, pick from, and ask what needs to happen to get there (S073).
- **Parts:** Twelve distinctives: bring people together, value all people, acknowledge their humanness, do the right things for the right reasons, give more than they take, develop emotional capacity, place people above their own agenda, embrace authenticity, take accountability, live by the bigger picture, do not keep score, desire the best for others (S084). S095 lists eleven of these. Jesus the High Road Leader builds on eight of them and roots two in the Gospels: Jesus valued all people and did not keep score (S085).
- **When to use:** Culture decisions, how to treat a competitor or critic, partnerships, and any choice where you could win by making the other side lose.
- **How to score (guidance):** High road if the decision gives more than it takes and does not keep score. Middle road if it is a fair trade and nothing more. Low road if it takes value from people or is about the founder's image.
- **Quote:** "the high road adds value to people just like a low road takes value from people" (S086) "I'll treat you okay if you treat me okay" (S086), his picture of the middle road.
- **Sources:** 12 (S012, S013, S029, S030, S063, S068, S073, S077, S084, S085, S086, S095)

## 7. Leadership is influence, and the Law of the Lid (everything rises and falls on leadership)
- **What it is:** Leadership is influence, not a title. A leader's ability is the lid on everyone they lead; when the leader grows, the lid lifts for the whole team. He put the Law of the Lid first in The 21 Irrefutable Laws of Leadership on purpose, to prove that leadership decides success (S076).
- **Parts:** Find who really has influence, as he did with the farmer his first board followed (S077). Rate yourself as a leader 1 to 10; the organization rarely rises above that (S022, S024, S054). Grow yourself to lift the lid (S006, S022). He calls his own role being a lid lifter (S066).
- **When to use:** When a company is stuck and the founder is looking outside for the cause; when deciding whether the founder's growth or a key leader's growth is the real bottleneck.
- **How to score (guidance):** Ask what number the founder's leadership is on a 1 to 10 scale and whether the plan needs a higher one. If the plan needs an 8 and the leader is a 5, the plan fails unless the leader grows or a stronger leader joins.
- **Quote:** "leadership is influence. Nothing more, nothing less." (S077) "leaders are always compelled to grow because they know that they set the lid for the people they lead." (S006) "how well you lead determines how well you succeed" (S053)
- **Sources:** 12 (S006, S022, S024, S053, S054, S060, S066, S072, S076, S077, S082, S103)

## 8. The Law of Connection (connect before you correct; get over yourself)
- **What it is:** From the 21 Laws and The 16 Undeniable Laws of Communication. Relationships are the first step of influence: people will not go along with you if they cannot get along with you. Connection starts where the other person sits, not where you stand, and you cannot think about yourself and them at the same time.
- **Parts:** Get over yourself so you can give yourself to them (S059, S065, S080). Connect, do not correct (S055, S087, S091). Four levels of relationships: surface, structured, secure, solid; how people handle conflict decides the level, and unhandled conflict drops it back a level (S044). Close comes first: impact people close up, impress them far away (S069). Best message and big message: ask what you want people to see, know, feel, and do; his answers are see possibilities, know I value them, feel empowered, apply and multiply (S008, S059, S066, S070).
- **When to use:** Onboarding, messaging, any content, and any decision about how a product talks to people.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the experience starts from what the user feels and needs right now. Low if it starts from what the founder wants to say or sell.
- **Quote:** "people won't go along with you if they can't get along with you" (S044) "are we going to connect with people or are we going to correct people you see connecting's the key" (S091)
- **Sources:** 13 (S008, S015, S044, S055, S059, S062, S065, S066, S069, S070, S080, S087, S091)

## 9. Failing forward and the return on failure (good miss versus bad miss)
- **What it is:** Failure is not the opposite of success; the two belong together. Failure beside success teaches humility; success beside failure teaches resilience. The failure itself is not what matters. Your response, adjust or make excuses, decides whether you get a return.
- **Parts:** Cycle of success: test, fail, learn, improve, re-enter, repeat (S074). Good miss (adjust) versus bad miss (excuses) (S090, S099). Upfront expectations: know before a new role that you will fail (S068). Five ways people respond to failure: blow up, cover up, speed up, back up, give up; almost nobody confesses up (S039). 24-hour rule: celebrate or grieve for 24 hours, then back to today (S067).
- **When to use:** After a failed launch, a bad hire, or a lost season; when deciding whether to relaunch, retry, or quit.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the team can name what it learned and what it changed before going back in. Low if it relaunches unchanged or blames the market. His bar: learning does not count unless you improve (S067, S072, S074).
- **Quote:** "It's your response to the failure that makes it either good or bad." (S076) "you don't get back in the game until you've improved" (S074)
- **Changed over time:** Failing Forward (2000) framed failure as a step toward success; Sometimes You Win, Sometimes You Learn (2013) made learning the point. Newer: How to Get a Return on Failure (2026) adds the good miss and bad miss test and says failure pays in lessons, character, and grace (S090, S097, S099, all 2026).
- **Sources:** 12 (S039, S058, S067, S068, S072, S074, S076, S090, S093, S095, S097, S099)

## 10. Seeds, not harvest (contribution bigger than reward; open heart, open mind, open hand)
- **What it is:** Judge a day by the seeds you sow, not the harvest you reap; the harvest is automatic if the seed is sown. He credits the seed line to Robert Louis Stevenson (S060). Make your contribution bigger than your reward, and do not give to get.
- **Parts:** No entitlement, and be the first to help (S014, ideas he takes from Zig Ziglar). Three kinds of generosity: open heart, open mind (always thinking how to help), open hand (money passes through) (S016, S088). Exchange principle: leave more than you take (S088, S089). Pick a number for significance, not security; if it is mine I accumulate, if it is his I invest (S075). Four questions before he gives: do the leaders have competence and character, can I give more than money, is it making a difference, what is God blessing (S096).
- **When to use:** Pricing, free tiers, generosity budgets, giving decisions, and any plan where the founder is designing the reward before the contribution.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if what the user gets clearly exceeds what they pay and the free layer is real help. Low if the reward is engineered first and the value is thin.
- **Quote:** "I consider the success of my day based on the seeds I sow not the Harvest I reap" (S060) "If I think it's mine, I accumulate. If I think it's his, I invest." (S075)
- **Sources:** 11 (S014, S016, S033, S060, S068, S074, S075, S088, S089, S096, S105)

## 11. Motivation versus manipulation (mutual advantage)
- **What it is:** Moving people for mutual advantage is motivation and leadership. Moving people for your own advantage is manipulation. His motive check before any decision: why am I doing this, and who am I trying to serve? Leaders see more and see before others; the test is whether they use that advantage for themselves or for the people (S007, S013, S082).
- **Parts:** Contrasts: motivation cares about results and people, manipulation only results; love versus ego; substance versus style; empowers versus controls; loyalty versus resentment (S013). The question he asks heads of state in private: who benefits the most, you or the people (S076, S102).
- **When to use:** Growth tactics (streaks, guilt notifications, fake urgency, upsells), sales scripts, and incentive plans.
- **How to score (guidance):** His own threshold: anything that does not serve others at least as much as it serves me is manipulation (S013). Score high if the user benefits at least as much as the company; low if the company is the main winner.
- **Quote:** "Motivation is for mutual advantage and manipulation is for personal my advantage." (S013) "Anything that's not serving others, at least as much as it's serving me, is manipulation." (S013)
- **Sources:** 10 (S007, S009, S013, S031, S051, S060, S069, S076, S082, S102)

## 12. Skills plus values (what makes leadership rise) *(unnamed as a law)*
- **What it is:** Leadership rises on two things together: competence and good values. Skills without values become manipulation. Values without skills mean people love you but cannot follow you anywhere. He added values training to EQUIP after training leaders who had skills but no values (S075).
- **Parts:** Competence (can you lead) and character (good values, the Golden Rule as the standard) (S019, S030, S062). Ethics needs a standard and the will to do it (S019). When he gives, he asks if the leaders have both, and says never substitute one for the other (S096).
- **When to use:** Choosing a cofounder, hire, partner, ministry to fund, or leader to follow.
- **How to score (guidance):** Rate competence and character 1 to 5 each. A low score on either is a no, however high the other is.
- **Quote:** "what causes leadership to rise is good leadership skills and good values and you have to have both of them you can't substitute one or the other" (S060)
- **Sources:** 8 (S019, S030, S060, S062, S064, S075, S096, S102)

## 13. Success versus significance (takers versus makers)
- **What it is:** Success is about me; significance is about others. He says too much success makes people selfish and that he has never met an unhappy person living for significance (S060). His definition of success, set in his mid 30s: those who know me best love and respect me the most (S060, S066).
- **Parts:** Three choices decide the road: motives (who you live for), maturity (accepting responsibility), mindset (taker or maker) (S031). Takers play defense and think win-lose; makers create, give, and think win-win (S031). Three-part success: know your purpose, grow to your potential, sow seeds that benefit others (S031, S041).
- **When to use:** Founder life decisions, growth plans that cost the family or team, and any choice framed only as winning.
- **How to score (guidance):** A plan that grows the company while the people closest to the founder trust and respect them less fails his definition, whatever the numbers say.
- **Quote:** "success is kind of about me but significance is about others" (S060) "success can be defined in the fact that those who know me the best love and respect me the most" (S060)
- **Sources:** 8 (S020, S031, S041, S060, S065, S066, S083, S100)

## 14. Five steps of equipping, and work yourself out of a job (add versus multiply)
- **What it is:** Leading followers adds; leading leaders multiplies. Every person is hired to work themselves out of their job and then get a bigger one. Every project starts with the question, who will I hand this to? He calls the multiplying return Maxwell math (S067).
- **Parts:** I do it; I do it and you are with me; you do it and I am with you; you do it; you do it and someone is with you (S053, S072, S073). He never equips anyone who will not commit to equip someone else (S053). No promotion without a trained replacement (S072). Hand off to potential, not experience, and at full speed, not when tired (S023, S067).
- **When to use:** Delegation, founder stepping back, promotions, and a founder who is the bottleneck.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the handoff has a named person moving through the five steps and the founder can name who that person will train. Low if the founder hands off cold or holds on. Caution from him: when a young founder wanted to step back, he said it was too early, because at that stage the team draws its energy from the founder (S073).
- **Quote:** "to add growth lead followers to multiply growth lead leaders" (S024) "multiplication of leaders is in that last step of you do it somebody's with you" (S073)
- **Sources:** 8 (S023, S024, S046, S053, S058, S067, S072, S073)

## 15. Hiring for values and fit (the four Cs and the values test)
- **What it is:** Shared values define the team (the Law of Identity, from The 17 Indisputable Laws of Teamwork). Talent alone does not guarantee results; the right values do. He hires for values first, then lets the team test the fit.
- **Parts:** The four Cs: calling (what are we called to do), chemistry (do these people work together), culture (do we nourish a leadership environment), challenge (are we growing) (S036). A values test before anyone joins (S023, S048). Let the team spend time with the candidate and report back (S023, S036, S060); his shortcut is to play golf and go to a buffet with them (S067). Two hiring tests: humility, seen in whether they ask questions, and growth potential (S068). The two L's for hiring and firing: love what you do and do not be lazy (S076). Superstars versus stars: a true 10 makes a place for themselves; when building, take the best person first (S050).
- **When to use:** First hires, cofounders, volunteer leaders, and any fire decision.
- **How to score (guidance):** Pass only if values match, the team says yes after real time together, and the person shows humility and room to grow. Skill without these is a no.
- **Quote:** "the law of identity says shared values Define the team" (S048) "I think the most important quality is humility." (S068)
- **Sources:** 8 (S023, S036, S048, S050, S060, S067, S068, S076)

## 16. The sweet spot (the Law of the Niche, the top five percent, the leader as quarterback)
- **What it is:** Position beats talent and commitment. Every player has a spot where they do their best work; the coach's job is to find it and design plays for it, and the player's job is to get there (his reading of John Wooden). You only reach the top five percent of a field in your area of giftedness, never by working on weaknesses.
- **Parts:** Work on strengths in the area of giftedness and on weaknesses in the area of choices, and do not confuse the two (S041). Lead in your strengths and follow in your weaknesses (S082). The leader is a quarterback who puts the ball in the right hands (S050). Know the team, the situation, and nine things about each player (S050).
- **When to use:** Role design, reorganizing, deciding who owns what, and a founder doing work outside their gift.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if each key person spends most of their time in their strength zone. Low if the plan depends on people fixing weaknesses to succeed.
- **Quote:** "you can't get to the top five working on weaknesses" (S054) "no matter how gifted you are no matter how committed you are if you're out of position you're not going to be successful" (S054)
- **Sources:** 7 (S041, S050, S054, S061, S062, S074, S082)

## 17. The Law of Magnetism and the Law of the Inner Circle (the 1 to 10 scale)
- **What it is:** Who you are is who you attract. A 5 attracts 5s and below; become an 8 and you attract 8s. Leaders follow people who are stronger leaders than they are (the Law of Respect). The people closest to you set the level of your success.
- **Parts:** Five ways to attract leaders: love what you do, be successful, take action, have a big vision, lift your leadership lid (S022). Watch who is coming and going: are 8s and 9s coming in and 2s and 3s leaving, or the reverse (S060). Choose the inner circle for values, influence, giftedness, and fit (S023, S037, S041).
- **When to use:** Recruiting senior people, picking advisors, and reading what turnover says about the leader.
- **How to score (guidance):** If strong people are leaving and weaker ones joining, read it as a verdict on the leader first.
- **Quote:** "you attract who you are not who you want" (S053) "those closest to you will determine the level of your success" (S037)
- **Sources:** 7 (S022, S023, S024, S037, S041, S053, S060)

## 18. The three questions every follower asks (care, help, trust)
- **What it is:** Every follower asks a leader three questions: do you care for me, can you help me, can I trust you. He gave them to ambassadors at the United Nations as common ground across governments (S053, S102). They can only be answered face to face, not through a book (S064).
- **Parts:** Care, competence, trust. He tells leaders to ask the three questions of themselves first (S076).
- **When to use:** Any product, team, or community a person is asked to join or stay in. For an app, the user asks the same three questions of the product.
- **How to score (guidance):** Score yes or no on each. A no on care sinks the rest, because people do not care how much you know until they know how much you care, a line he repeats and credits to his founding pastor (S076, S101).
- **Quote:** "do you care for me can you help me and can I trust you" (S030) "people really don't care how much you know until they know how much you care." (S076)
- **Sources:** 7 (S030, S053, S062, S064, S076, S101, S102)

## 19. The three Rs of prioritizing (required, return, reward)
- **What it is:** Pick your work by what is required of you, what gives the greatest return, and what gives you the greatest reward. He has used it since about 1974 and teaches it in Developing the Leader Within You 2.0. Return keeps the company winning, required keeps you in your lane, reward restores your energy (S073).
- **Parts:** Score each decision 1 to 3 for payoff and 1 to 3 for fit with your goals; totals of two or three need attention (S034). Pick one main event each day (S034). For anything else, ask not can I do it, but can someone else do it (S071). He keeps asking, where is the return (S078).
- **When to use:** Founder calendar, what the founder keeps versus delegates, and which project gets the team's best hours.
- **How to score (guidance):** Keep it if it scores high on required and return; delegate if someone else can do it; drop it if it scores low on all three.
- **Quote:** "what's required of me what gives me the greatest return and what brings me the greatest reward" (S034) "not can I do it but can someone else do it" (S071)
- **Sources:** 7 (S034, S053, S058, S071, S073, S074, S078)

## 20. Grow the people to grow the organization (seven growth practices)
- **What it is:** The most effective way to grow an organization is to grow its people; the organization cannot outgrow them. Love everyone unconditionally, but give your development time to those who earn it.
- **Parts:** Seven practices: develop a game plan, put yourself in the growth picture, put your people in it, create a growth culture, connect with them, give them a challenging project, empower them (S040). A challenging project tests emotional, creative, and ability capacity (S040). Give most teaching time to the top producers and let them train the rest (the Pareto principle, S024, and Gladwell's law of the few as he uses it, S040). Developing people means valuing, committing time, mentoring, equipping, and empowering (S082).
- **When to use:** Training budgets, team growth plans, and where the founder spends people time.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the plan names who will grow, how, and who models it first. Low if training is an event and the leaders skip it; he can tell on day one that a program will fail when the top does not go through it (S062, S064).
- **Quote:** "The most effective way to grow an organization is to grow the people in it. That's a fact." (S040) "you don't have to earn my love, I give that to you unconditionally. But you do have to earn my time." (S069)
- **Sources:** 7 (S024, S040, S043, S048, S062, S069, S082)

## 21. Making the tough call (eight helps, the decision puzzle, decision managing)
- **What it is:** Tough calls come with leadership. Do the homework, set a deadline, get the timing right, take counsel from the right people, and never break your values to make the call. A good decision only becomes great through how you manage it afterward.
- **Parts:** Eight helps: accept tough calls, do your homework, set a deadline, check the timing, seek the right counsel, decide on your principles, build systems, understand the emotions (S037). The decision puzzle: define the issue in writing, gather and weigh information, question your first impressions, outline a strategy (S037). Ten traps, from procrastinating to not tracking results (S035). PGE: weigh the pains, the gains, and your experience (S056). Put the people first and you can live with the decision (S054).
- **When to use:** Firing, cutting a program, a pivot, or any call the founder keeps putting off.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the issue is written down, the deadline is set, the right people were asked, values were not traded, and results will be tracked. When pushed to decide in a hurry, his default is no.
- **Quote:** "accept tough calls as a requirement of leadership just start there" (S037) "no is more easily changed to yes than yes is changed to no" (S035)
- **Sources:** 5 (S034, S035, S037, S054, S056)

## 22. Leading through crisis (people first, define reality, show up with hope)
- **What it is:** A crisis does not make a leader; it reveals one. His first move is always to put the people first. Then define reality, adjust like a coach at halftime, and help people get traction in the middle of distraction.
- **Parts:** Seven moves: put the people first, educate yourself, be flexible, leverage your team, communicate wisely more than constantly, be authentic, manage yourself (S056). Show up early, with clarity and with hope; hope is active, optimism is passive (a line he credits to Jonathan Sacks in S056 and repeats in S089). Problems versus facts of life: put behind you what you cannot do and before you what you can (from his mentor Fred Smith, S001). Three questions: how will this make me better, how will I use it to help others, what action will I take (S057).
- **When to use:** Cash crunch, public failure, layoffs, or a hard season for users.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the response tells the truth, admits what is unknown, and protects people first. Low if the leader pretends to have answers.
- **Quote:** "Crisis don't make us. Crisis reveal us." (S056) "I don't know if there's anything more disgusting than a leader that's pretending to have answers during difficult times, uncertain times." (S056)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S010, S056, S057, S093)

## 23. The 5 Levels of Leadership
- **What it is:** His step-by-step map of influence. People follow at level 1 because they have to (position), at 2 because they want to (permission, relationships), at 3 because of what you have done for the organization (production), at 4 because of what you have done for them (people development), at 5 because of who you are (pinnacle, respect). Each level stands on the one below, and each is harder than the last.
- **Parts:** Level 2 leaders listen, observe, and serve (S053). Level 3 leaders lead by example, create momentum, and attract better people (S053). Level 4 leaders recruit, position, and equip (S053). Friendship at level 2 is not enough; you have to produce (S072). Respect is earned on difficult ground (S073).
- **When to use:** Judging why a team follows a founder, why a new manager is struggling, or what a leader must do next.
- **How to score (guidance):** Place the founder on the level each key person follows them from. A founder leading mostly from position (level 1) needs relationships before asking for more; one stuck at 3 needs to start developing others.
- **Quote:** "level one is where we start it's good place to start bad place to stay" (S053) "the position doesn't make the leader the leader makes the position" (S053)
- **Sources:** 4 (S029, S053, S072, S073)

## 24. The three E's of developing leaders (environment, equipping, exposure)
- **What it is:** Environment is incarnational: leaders do what they experience, so leadership is caught more than taught. Equipping is intentional: leaders do what they learn. Exposure is inspirational: leaders do what they see. The function of leadership is to produce more leaders, not more followers.
- **Parts:** Equipping starts with three expectations: leadership determines growth, leadership can be learned, each leader equips leaders (S046). Watch one, do one, teach one, borrowed from the emergency room (S046). Exposure through his monthly learning lunch with someone further along, with seven prepared questions (S041, S046, S071, S093). The leadership table: invite everyone to talk leadership and watch who leans in (S072, S073).
- **When to use:** Building a leadership pipeline in a small company, church, or volunteer team.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if emerging leaders get to practice leading on real projects. He finds leaders by letting them practice for about three months (S073).
- **Quote:** "And the function of leadership is very simple, it's to produce more leaders, not more followers." (S046) "a leadership table is not an official position but it's a possibility position" (S073)
- **Sources:** 4 name or apply the three E's and the leadership table (S046, S072, S073, S089); the learning lunch appears in 6 more (S041, S071, S074, S082, S093, S100)

## 25. Leadership rhythm: push, pull, be patient
- **What it is:** Three moves used together. Push is telling, aimed at performance and choices, delivered with candor. Pull is asking, aimed at development and gifts, delivered with compassion. Patience is accepting it may take five pushes. You have to know the person first to know which one they need.
- **Parts:** Push on matters of choice: values, deadlines, responsibility, production, commitment, attitude. Pull on strengths and gifts by asking. Lead each person differently (S072).
- **When to use:** Coaching an underperformer, a team member with untapped gifts, or a founder who only knows one mode.
- **How to score (guidance):** If the gap is a choice (effort, attitude, deadlines), push. If it is an undeveloped gift, pull. If the leader is doing only one, the rhythm is off.
- **Quote:** "And and by the way, in in in matters of choice, I push people." (S028) "everybody the same. Whereas leaders understand that you lead everyone differently." (S072)
- **Sources:** 2 (S028, S072)

## Tensions and limits in these frameworks
- **Love everyone, but give time to the few.** He says value all people and never pick and choose (S012), and also says give most teaching time to the top 20 percent (S024). His own resolution: love is unconditional, time is earned (S069).
- **Relationship first or respect first?** The 5 Levels put relationships (level 2) before production (S053). For secular and business rooms he now says you start with respect, not relationship (S066, 2023; S075, 2026; S077, 2026). Inferred from S053, S066, S075: inside a team, connect first; with strangers and skeptics, earn respect by being good first.
- **He over-cares.** He admits he gives people too many chances and asks his team to raise a hand when he does: "I tell my team there are times I care for you a little unhealthy." (S076)
- **He is not good at all of his own laws.** He says he is average or worse at five of the 21 laws, which is why he leans on a team (S054, S082).
- **Thin on numbers.** His frameworks are built for leading people in churches, nonprofits, and his own training companies. They say little about pricing, unit economics, product design, or competition; on money he talks giving, stewardship, and real reporting rather than margins (inferred from S016, S075, S088, S096).
- **Slogans repeat.** Many of his lines recur word for word across decades of talks. Repetition shows conviction, not proof that the idea fits a given decision.
